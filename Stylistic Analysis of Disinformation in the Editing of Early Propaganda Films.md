# Stylistic Analysis of Disinformation in the Editing of Early Propaganda Films

**Abstract**

This research investigates how editing techniques in early propaganda films generate misleading narratives. Using D.W. Griffith's *The Birth of a Nation* (1915), Sergei Eisenstein's *Battleship Potemkin* (1925), and Leni Riefenstahl's *Triumph of the Will* (1935) as case studies, the paper argues that ideological authority emerges through formal editing choices rather than content alone. Griffith employs parallel editing to aestheticize white supremacist violence; Eisenstein uses intellectual montage to present revolutionary ideology as logical inference; Riefenstahl integrates rhythmic editing with cinematography to normalize political spectacle. The study concludes that editing, the primary formal operation of cinema, is therefore the primary mechanism of cinematic disinformation, distinguishing stylistic analysis from ideological critique and offering practical implications for visual media literacy.
